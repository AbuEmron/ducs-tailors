# Brand guidelines

*Fi Sabilillah. The mark, what it means, and the rules that keep it meaning that.*

Every asset described here is generated by `python3 tools/brand/build.py` from one geometry
source and checked by `python3 tools/brand/validate.py`. Nothing under `brand/` is drawn by
hand, and every claim on this page is asserted by a check in that file — including the ones
that would be embarrassing to get wrong.

---

## 1. The identity

`fi sabilillah` means **in the path of Allah**. The identity reproduces the supplied
reference: a gold onion arch on deep green, carrying a crescent and star above the phrase
`في سبيل الله`, cradled by two leaf sweeps.

| Element | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| The arch | Masjid, shelter, community, sacred space. The silhouette the identity is recognised by. |
| The finial and keyline | Depth. One outline reads as a shape; an outline with a keyline reads as a doorway. |
| The crescent and star | Faith, guidance, divine purpose. |
| The calligraphy | Unity, deen, and a timeless Islamic identity — and the name of the thing, in the language it comes from. |
| The leaves | Service, help, positive impact. Hands cupped rather than a shape running off the bottom of the mark. |

Those five readings are the reference's own, from its "meaning behind the design" panel.
They are kept because they were the brief.

**What the mark must never be used to claim.** Religious authority, a scholarly ruling,
guaranteed reward, charitable registration, or that any organisation displaying it has been
vetted by anyone. The badges say what has been checked; the logo says only whose application
this is.

## 2. The Arabic

`في سبيل الله` — *fī sabīlillāh*. Real Unicode Arabic, in a real Arabic typeface (**Noto
Kufi Arabic**, SIL OFL 1.1), shaped by **HarfBuzz**. HarfBuzz chooses the contextual forms
and substitutes the ﷲ ligature; nothing in this system picks a glyph by hand, and nothing
draws a shape that is meant to look like Arabic.

This matters more than it sounds. The brief rules out "fake or malformed Arabic calligraphy"
twice, and is right to: script that approximates Arabic without resolving into words is the
one thing a Muslim audience notices first and forgives last. The answer was never to omit
the Arabic — it was to set it correctly.

`validate.py` asserts three things about it, every build:

- that the shaper produced **contextual forms** rather than isolated letters;
- that `الله` resolved to the **ﷲ ligature** (`uniFDF2`) rather than four separate letters;
- that the path in the exported icon is **byte-identical** to what re-shaping produces now,
  so nobody can hand-edit the calligraphy without the build failing.

Set on two lines — `في سبيل` over `الله` — because on one line it is a 6:1 run, and inside a
field of the arch's proportions that means a scale where the strokes are a hairline. Two
lines is an ordinary way to set the phrase compactly, keeps every letter in its correct
form, and reads in the right order.

**Below 32px there is no calligraphy at all.** At 24px the strokes are a fifth of a pixel
and it becomes a grey smear inside the arch. Illegible Arabic is precisely what this section
exists to prevent, so the small cut drops it and enlarges the crescent to fill the field.

**If Arabic is ever added anywhere else** it must be real Unicode Arabic in a shaped Arabic
typeface, and it must be read by a native reader before it ships. No Quranic text is to be
used in any mark, at any size, for any variant.

## 3. Colour

Two palettes, on purpose. The **brand** palette is what the logo, the app icon, the splash,
the banners and the store listing are painted in. The **product** palette in
`androidApp/.../ui/theme/Color.kt` is what the application's screens are painted in, and it
is the Amanah harbour blue. They meet in three places: splash, landing, app icon.

Forcing one to be the other makes both worse. The green is too dark to carry interface text
at the contrast a UI needs; the harbour blue does not read as an institution at 60px on a
shelf of app icons.

### Brand

The five values the reference names are kept exactly.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `forest-deep` | `#0A2A24` | (10, 42, 36) | 168.8° 61.5% 10.2% | 76/0/14/84 | The field inside the arch, and the splash. |
| `mineral` | `#1C574A` | (28, 87, 74) | 166.8° 51.3% 22.5% | 68/0/15/66 | Raised surfaces on green; the halo behind a banner. |
| `sage` | `#6BAA7D` | (107, 170, 125) | 137.1° 27.0% 54.3% | 37/0/26/33 | The tagline. Large text only — see below. |
| `gold` | `#D4AF37` | (212, 175, 55) | 45.9° 64.6% 52.4% | 0/17/74/17 | The mark, the rule, the pillars. Dark grounds only. |

### The gold ramp

The metal is a four-stop gradient, not a flat fill and not a bevel filter:

| Stop | HEX | At |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highlight | `#F0DFA8` | 0% |
| Light | `#E0C264` | 28% |
| Core | `#D4AF37` | 64% |
| Shadow | `#A8811F` | 100% |

Run on a diagonal (`x1 .12 y1 0 → x2 .62 y2 1`), so the light falls on the arch's upper left
the way the reference lights it. The keyline inside the band is drawn in `#F4E6BB`, which is
where that light lands.

**A gradient rather than a bevel or a lighting filter.** A filter does not survive being
printed, foil-blocked, converted to a VectorDrawable, or rendered at 16px — and every one of
those happens to this mark. `fi-sabilillah-icon-flat.svg` is the same shape with the
gradient collapsed to one gold, for silkscreen, embroidery and single-plate print.

**Every VectorDrawable is flat.** A VectorDrawable *can* express a gradient, but Android's
themed-icon layer composites the whole foreground in a single tint, and a two-tone foreground
becomes a solid blob under it.

### Gold is an accent

The reference's rule, and it is enforced by construction: gold is never the ground. It is the
mark, the rule, the pillars line, and nothing else. Deep green carries every surface; ivory
carries every word. An asset where gold is the largest area of colour is wrong, whatever it
looks like.

### Functional

Restated from the product palette so nobody reading only this sheet invents a second success
green.

| Token | HEX | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `success` | `#3E7A5E` | Confirmed, settled, attended. |
| `warning` | `#AF7A2A` | Needs attention. Never used for danger. |
| `danger` | `#A04B55` | Restriction, dispute, refusal. |
| `learning` | `#6C638E` | Learning surfaces, and nothing else. |
| `service` | `#246F6C` | Service and safeguard-affirmative. |
| `neutral` | `#68757A` | Everything unweighted. |

### Contrast, measured

| Pair | Ratio | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ivory on deep green | **10.80:1** | AA for text |
| gold on deep green | **5.75:1** | AA for text |
| gold on ivory | **1.88:1** | FAILS — never use this pair |
| gold-deep on ivory | **5.45:1** | AA for text |
| deep green on ivory | **10.80:1** | AA for text |
| ivory on charcoal | **16.93:1** | AA for text |
| sage on deep green | **4.42:1** | AA **large only** — the tagline, and nothing else |

**Gold never touches a light background.** `gold` on `ivory` is 1.88:1, which is illegible.
Light grounds use `gold-deep`. Every generated light-tone asset already does; the failure
mode is a designer picking the gold swatch by eye in a new document.

`validate.py` asserts the floors *and* asserts that gold-on-ivory still fails — because if it
ever passed, the rule sending light grounds to `gold-deep` would have gone stale and nobody
would have noticed.

**Sage is a large-text colour.** 4.42:1 clears AA for text at 18.66px bold or 24px regular
and does not clear it below that. It carries "For the Sake of Allah" in every lockup, where
it is set at display size. It is not a body colour and there is a check that keeps it above
the 3:1 large-text floor.

## 4. Typography

The reference names its own faces, and both are used exactly as named.

| Role | Face | Licence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display — the wordmark and tagline | **Playfair Display** | SIL OFL 1.1 | The reference's own. Elegant, timeless, trustworthy. |
| Text — the pillars line and labels | **Inter** | SIL OFL 1.1 | The reference's own. Clean, modern, highly readable. |
| The emblem's calligraphy | **Noto Kufi Arabic** | SIL OFL 1.1 | A real Arabic typeface, shaped by a real shaper. |
| The interface | The Amanah scale in `ui/theme/Type.kt` | — | Already in place; the brand does not override it. |

All three are vendored into `tools/brand/fonts/` and all three licences travel with the
repository under `brand/licences/`. The OFL permits embedding and permits deriving outlines;
what it forbids — selling the font software, reusing a reserved name — does not apply to a
logotype.

**Outlines, not font references.** A font-referencing SVG depends on that font being
installed wherever the file is opened: an app-store listing, a partner's deck, a printer's
RIP. Where it is not, the wordmark silently falls back to Times New Roman and nobody notices
until it is printed. Every letterform in every asset is a path. `validate.py` rejects any SVG
containing `<text`, `font-family` or `@font-face`.

**Setting.** The wordmark is capitals at 0.17em tracking. The tagline is Playfair Regular at
0.06em. The pillars line is Inter SemiBold at 0.24em. The type scale, in multiples of the
wordmark's cap height:

| Line | Scale |
| --- | --- |
| `FI SABILILLAH` | 1.00 |
| `For the Sake of Allah` | 0.30 |
| `SERVE ◆ LEARN ◆ GROW ◆ GIVE` | 0.17 |
| Labels | 0.13 |

## 5. Construction

The emblem is drawn on a 64-unit grid.

**Symmetry is structural.** Every arch is authored as its right-hand half and mirrored by
`geometry._mirror`. Typing both halves is how a mark ends up 0.1 units lopsided three
revisions later, in a way nobody can see and everybody can feel. There is a check that each
arch's bounding box is centred on x=32 to within 10⁻⁶.

**The ink box is computed, not typed.** The band's stroke pushes past the silhouette by half
its width and the leaf tips run wider and lower than the arch, so the mark occupies
39.5 × 55.3 of its 64-unit square rather than filling it. Every layout aligns to that box.
A hardcoded box is correct exactly until somebody nudges a leaf.

- **Clear space**: one arch-stroke width on all four sides — 4 units on the 64-grid, 7.2% of
  the emblem's height. Nothing enters it, including the wordmark.
- **Minimum size, emblem**: 16px.
- **Minimum size, horizontal lockup**: 180px wide. The wordmark is 11.6 cap-heights long.
- **Simplification is automatic**, at 32px, in every implementation.

### The small cut

Below 32px the mark drops the keyline, the calligraphy and the curled leaf tips, thickens the
band from 4.0 to 5.2, and enlarges the crescent and star to fill the field that the
calligraphy has left. Detail you cannot resolve does not read as detail — it reads as dirt
over the parts that were legible.

`BrandEmblem` in Compose and `FiSabilillahLogo` in React both decide this from the size they
are handed. Leaving it to the caller means it gets got wrong exactly once, in a notification,
at 24dp.

### Android adaptive icon

The foreground occupies **50% of the 108dp viewport**, not the 62% that looks right in a
preview. Android guarantees only the central **66dp circle**; everything outside it is the
launcher's to crop, and every OEM crops it differently. The mark is a tall arch with leaves
at the foot, so its furthest-from-centre points are the leaf tips — at 62% they sit 40.6dp
out, which is comfortably inside a square mask and clipped by a circular one.

`validate.py` samples 800 points along every path, projects them into the viewport, and fails
if any lands outside 33dp. It samples the curves rather than the bounding box, because the
mark's bbox corners are empty and a corner check would reject an icon that is comfortably
inside the circle.

The foreground carries **no baked corner radius and no clip** — the launcher applies the
mask, and a pre-rounded foreground gets rounded twice. The gold hairline the reference insets
inside the plate lives only on `fi-sabilillah-icon-presentation.svg`, for the same reason.

## 7. Badges and pillars

Two different things, and confusing them would be a real error.

**Badges** — seven, sharing the emblem's own arch closed and filled. A badge asserts that
something was checked.

| Badge | Glyph | States |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `verified-organization` | A check | Registration documents were seen by a named reviewer. |
| `learning` | An open book | A learning offering. Not an endorsement of its content. |
| `service` | The emblem's arch | A service opportunity. |
| `community` | Three equal dots | A community space. Unranked, deliberately. |
| `safety` | A lock | Privacy and restriction surfaces. |
| `donation` | Given into cupped hands | A campaign that has passed financial review. |
| `family-introduction` | Two parties under an arch | A formal introduction, with a guardian present. |

A badge never states that something is religiously approved, safe, or guaranteed.

**Pillars** — five line icons, the reference's bottom row. They assert nothing; they label a
section.

| Pillar | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Community | We rise together |
| Knowledge | Learn. Implement. Grow. |
| Service | Help with Ikhlas |
| Giving | Sadaqah & donations |
| Safety | Built on Trust & Amanah |

## 8. Motion

One gesture, 520ms, then still: the mark fades up and settles from 96% to 100% on
`cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1)` — the same curve the rest of the Amanah system uses.

Forbidden without exception: spinning, flashing, looping, confetti, particles, glow pulses,
shimmer passes across the gold, and any animation that plays on completing an act of service.
A record of service is between a person and their Lord; animating it turns it into a
performance.

Under reduced motion the animation is **removed, not shortened**.

## 9. Incorrect use

Do not:

- stretch, squash or rotate the mark;
- recolour it outside the tones in §6;
- put `gold` on a light background — it is 1.88:1;
- make gold the ground, or the largest area of colour in any asset;
- set the tagline's sage at body size;
- apply the gold gradient to a VectorDrawable, a monochrome cut, or anything that will be
  tinted;
- add a dome, minaret or second building to it — the arch is the building this identity owns;
- alter, redraw, replace or hand-edit the Arabic, or add any other Arabic to any variant;
- place it over busy photography without a plate behind it;
- alter the leaf sweeps, the keyline or the stroke weights individually;
- add a glow, bevel, drop shadow or a second metallic gradient;
- extend the adaptive foreground past the 66dp safe circle;
- use the full lockup as a repeating element in the interface;
- imply with it that anything is religiously approved, or that the platform vouches for a
  person or an organisation.

## 10. Accessibility

- Every generated SVG carries `role="img"` and an `aria-label`; decorative instances pass
  `title={null}` / `contentDescription = null` and are hidden from assistive technology
  rather than announced twice.
- The mark never relies on colour to be understood: the monochrome variants are first-class
  exports, not fallbacks.
- The wordmark is drawn as paths and does not scale with the system font setting. That is
  correct for a logo and correct nowhere else — every sentence in the application still
  scales.
- Contrast ratios are measured, recorded in §3, and asserted in `validate.py`.
- RTL: the horizontal lockup mirrors as a whole (emblem to the right of the wordmark). The
  emblem itself is **not** mirrored — it is symmetrical, and the Arabic inside it must not be
  reversed.

## 11. Legal

- Trademark status: **none registered.** `Fi Sabilillah` is a common Arabic phrase and may be
  difficult to register as a word mark in some jurisdictions; the emblem is the stronger
  candidate. Take advice before filing.
- **The emblem is drawn from a reference image supplied for this work, at that supplier's
  direction.** Every path is original geometry — computed circle intersections for the
  crescent, mirrored cubics for the arch, HarfBuzz output for the Arabic — and no part of the
  reference is traced, embedded or reproduced in any exported file. But the design is
  deliberately close to it, which is a different position from independent creation, and
  anyone filing should say so.
- A crescent and star are, separately, a very common device. That helps nothing on
  distinctiveness: the arch, the calligraphy, the leaf cradle and the lockup are what a
  filing would rest on.
- Three OFL typefaces are used, with their licences in `brand/licences/`. No stock artwork
  and no commercially licensed typeface is embedded in any asset.
- **Scholarly review is recommended before public launch**, on three points: the use of the
  phrase `fi sabilillah` as a product name, the setting of `في سبيل الله` inside a commercial
  mark, and the absence of any claim of religious authority in the marks and badges. This
  document asserts the third; a qualified scholar should confirm the first two.

## 12. Regenerating

Nothing under `brand/` is edited by hand.

```console
$ python3 tools/brand/build.py      # 99 files
$ python3 tools/brand/validate.py   # 369 checks
```

Geometry lives in `tools/brand/geometry.py`, letterforms and shaping in `letters.py`, colour
and type tokens in `tokens.py`. Change one of those and every export moves together — which
is the only way "all exports use consistent geometry" survives a second revision.

Build-time dependencies: `cairosvg`, `Pillow`, `fontTools`, `uharfbuzz`, `svgpathtools`. None
of them ship in the application; they exist so the assets that do ship can be generated
rather than drawn.

The Android app's `res/drawable` holds copies of six generated files, because that is where
`aapt` looks. `validate.py` asserts they are byte-identical to the generated originals, so a
build that forgets to re-copy them fails rather than shipping a stale mark.
